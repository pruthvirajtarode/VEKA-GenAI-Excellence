📎Handling Docs & Photos

Attaching a file doesn't replace a prompt — it just gives the prompt something real to point at.

✅ Documents — do this

  • Name the doc type and exactly what to pull out
  • Ask for a specific output shape — table, list, JSON
  • Point at the section that matters in a long file
  • Ask it to quote or cite the line it's working from

❌ Documents — avoid this

  • "Check this" with no definition of wrong
  • Dumping a 40-page doc with no pointer to what matters
  • Assuming it knows your company's specific terms
  • Skipping the format, then reformatting it yourself anyway

✅ Photos — do this

  • Say what's in frame that actually matters
  • Ask for structured output — a count, a list, yes/no
  • Give the business context a photo can't show
  • Ask it to flag anything it's unsure about

❌ Photos — avoid this

  • "What's in this picture?" with no goal
  • Trusting a count with zero double-check
  • Skipping context ("this is our warehouse shelf 4B")
  • One photo when the comparison needs a before/after pair

📚12 Ready-to-Paste Promptsyour library

Two per role. Copy, swap the bracketed bits, go.

📈 Sales / Business Dev

Follow-up after silence 
Act as an SDR. Write a 3-line follow-up email
to a lead who went quiet [X] days after a demo.
Remind them of [the specific value point we
discussed], no pressure tone, one clear CTA to
book 15 minutes. Keep it under 60 words.
Deal-risk summary 
Act as a sales manager. Read this deal thread
and tell me: (1) the single biggest risk to this
deal closing, (2) one question I should ask the
client this week, (3) a confidence score 1-10
with your reasoning in one sentence.

📣 Marketing / Content

Hook lines 
Act as a B2B content strategist. Give me 5
LinkedIn post hooks (1 line each) for [audience]
about [topic]. No emojis, no hashtags, punchy
not salesy. Rank them best to worst and say why
the top one works.
Repurpose long-form 
Act as an editor. Take this [blog post /
transcript] and pull out 3 standalone social
posts, each under 280 characters, that could
run without any extra context. Keep our voice:
direct, no buzzwords.

⚙️ Operations / PM

90-second status 
Act as a PMO lead. Summarize this project update
for a director with 90 seconds to read it:
- Status in 1 line
- 3 risks, each with an owner name
- 2 decisions needed this week
No fluff, no restating the obvious.
Meeting notes → actions 
Act as an ops coordinator. Turn these raw
meeting notes into an action list: owner, task,
due date. Flag anything mentioned but never
assigned to a person.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 HR / People

Feedback draft 
Act as a supportive manager. From these notes,
draft written feedback: 2 strengths, 1 growth
area. Warm but direct tone, under 150 words,
no corporate jargon like "synergy" or "leverage."
Job description 
Act as a recruiter. Write a job description for
a [role] on a [team size]-person team, [seniority]
level, must-have skills: [list]. Format as 5
sections with headings, under 400 words.

💰 Finance / Analytics

Anomaly check 
Act as a financial analyst. Compare [this
month's] expense sheet against [last month's].
List every line item that moved more than 15%,
as a table with % change. Flag anything that
looks like a possible duplicate.
Contract fee scan 
Act as a finance analyst reviewing a vendor
contract for hidden fees. List every fee found,
where in the doc it appears, and flag anything
you're not 100% sure about instead of guessing.
Format as a table.

🎧 Customer Support

De-escalation reply 
Act as a senior support agent. Write a reply
that acknowledges [the specific issue], offers
[the fix], and does NOT over-apologize. Under
100 words, calm and direct tone.
Ticket pattern check 
Act as a support lead. Read these 10 tickets
and tell me: the single most common root cause,
how many tickets it explains, and one process
fix that would prevent it next time.
🏁Final Check · 5 Questions+10 XP each
Q1 of 5
Which part of a prompt tells the AI how the output should look?
CorrectFormat is the shape of the output — table, bullets, word count.
Q2 of 5
What's the biggest risk of dumping a 40-page doc with no pointer to what matters?
CorrectWithout a pointer, it guesses what matters — and it usually guesses wrong.
Q3 of 5
When analyzing a photo, what should you always ask for?
CorrectStructured output plus an uncertainty flag is what makes a photo answer usable.
Q4 of 5
"Write a job description" with a role, seniority, and must-have skills — but no format — is missing what?
CorrectEverything else is there — without a format you'll get a wall of prose you have to restructure yourself.
Q5 of 5
Why give the AI a Role at all?
Correct"Act as a finance analyst" vs "act as a marketer" reading the same doc produce genuinely different, useful answers.
